Chapbook Privacy Policy

Last revised: 27 October 2026

Chapbook is a cipher game for iPhone built from sentences in old books. It is published on the App Store by G MARCA COMPANIA CREATIVA LTDA (Colombia), referred to here as "the developer". Privacy questions can be sent to contact@gmarcacompaniacreativaltda.com.

Short version

  • Chapbook has no sign-up, no server and no code that talks to the internet. Its library of phrases ships inside the app.
  • What you do in Chapbook is reported to no one, the developer included. The app carries no advertising, analytics or crash-reporting libraries, and it does not follow you into other apps or onto websites.
  • Personal information reaches the developer in a single way: an email that you decide to write.

Kept on your device

To let you put a phrase down and return to it later, Chapbook saves a little information in the storage area iOS sets aside for it on your iPhone:

  • Phrases in progress. One small file for each phrase you have started (for Today, one per date). It holds which phrase it is, the cipher it was dealt with, the letters you have placed, your pencil marks and the list of moves that Step back and Step forward walk through. For phrases opened from the library it also holds a random number drawn to build that cipher; the number says nothing about you or your device. A phrase's file is deleted when you solve it.
  • Collection and calendar. For every solved phrase: its number in the library, the calendar day of the solve and its position among your solves. Also the Today dates you completed, the last twenty phrases offered to you (so that Surprise me does not repeat itself) and whether the first-run tips have been shown.
  • Settings. Signs (Letters or Numbers), Starting letters, Check as you type, Appearance, Haptics, Larger page text and whether the on-screen keys are folded away.
  • Files that could not be read. Should a saved file become damaged, Chapbook puts it aside under a new name rather than writing over it, keeping no more than the three newest such copies.
  • Brief technical entries in the system log when a file cannot be saved or the phrase library cannot be loaded. They describe the fault, not your letters, and Chapbook sends the log nowhere.

None of this involves a clock. Chapbook notes on which day something happened, never the time of day or how long you played.

None of these files is visible to the developer.

Clearing it. Clear the page, in a phrase's menu, removes your letters and pencil marks for that phrase. Deleting Chapbook from your device erases everything listed above. Device backups to iCloud or to a Mac or PC, when you have them turned on, may contain these files as well; such copies stay in your iCloud account or on the computer that made them, where the developer has no access, and are governed by the backup you chose.

Never requested

Chapbook asks for no permissions at all. It does not use where you are, your photos, contacts, calendar, camera, microphone or health records. It sends no notifications, and since it tracks nothing, Apple's tracking prompt never appears.

Sharing a phrase

A solved phrase in your Collection can be shared. Chapbook passes only the words of the phrase and its attribution to the system share sheet. Where they go next is your choice, and the recipient's own rules apply from that point. Chapbook does not note that anything was shared, or where.

The two web pages

The Privacy Policy and Support links in About open in your browser. These pages are served by GitHub Pages. Like any web host, it receives the technical details a browser sends with each request, for example your IP address and browser type, and handles them under its own policy. The developer puts no analytics, cookies or advertising on these pages.

Writing to the developer

When you email contact@gmarcacompaniacreativaltda.com, the developer gets your sending address, the sender name if your mail service shows one, and whatever the message and its attachments contain. It is read in order to answer you and, if you point out a wrong word, author or date, to check that phrase against its source and correct it in a later version. Your address will not be used to send you anything except the reply, and the message is neither sold nor given away. The developer's inbox sits with a commercial mail host. When nothing more needs answering, the exchange is removed from the mailbox; you can ask for that to happen straight away.

If you are in Switzerland, the UK or the EEA, the basis is Article 6(1)(f) GDPR: a sender expects an answer, and giving one is a legitimate interest of the developer. The UK GDPR and Switzerland's data protection act work the same way here.

For a developer established in Colombia: anything a message reveals about you is personal data under Ley Estatutaria 1581 de 2012 (habeas data) and Decreto 1074 de 2015, and the developer is its responsable del tratamiento (controller). Emailing the developer is itself the authorisation for the narrow use described above, answering and checking a phrase, and nothing beyond it. The developer's mail host may keep messages on servers in other countries. At contact@gmarcacompaniacreativaltda.com, and without charge, you may ask what is held about you and how it has been used, have it brought up to date, corrected or erased, ask for evidence of the authorisation, or withdraw it. The law gives the developer 10 business days for a question and 15 for a claim, extendable as it provides. If the answer does not resolve the matter, a complaint can then go to the Superintendencia de Industria y Comercio, the authority for personal data in Colombia. The developer can also be reached on +57 350 261 0540, and by post at the address given at the top of this page.

Information Apple passes on

In App Store Connect the developer sees summary numbers, for instance how often Chapbook was installed in each storefront. If Share With App Developers is on (iOS Settings, then Privacy & Security, then Analytics & Improvements), Apple can also send reports of crashes along with statistics about how Chapbook is used. Apple gathers that material according to Apple's privacy policy and removes details that identify you. The developer reads it to locate bugs. Switching the setting off ends this.

Age

Chapbook offers no chat, sells nothing and has no place for players to post anything. Its phrases are published sentences by writers who died in 1925 or earlier, picked for wit and good sense and screened for violence, sexual content, drugs, gambling and swearing. The developer does not set out to gather anything from children; if a child has emailed the developer, the child's parent can have that email deleted by asking.

Your rights by law

Where you live may give you rights under privacy law, for instance the GDPR across the EU and EEA, the UK GDPR, the Swiss Federal Act on Data Protection or the California Consumer Privacy Act. Typically these let you ask a business which personal information it holds about you, obtain a copy, get mistakes in it fixed, have it deleted, have its use restricted or object to it, and bring the matter before a privacy regulator. In Chapbook's case the developer holds nothing about you apart from messages you chose to email. Requests of this kind go to contact@gmarcacompaniacreativaltda.com; an answer follows as quickly as the relevant law requires, and usually sooner. There is no selling of personal information, and none of it is used to target ads at you based on what you do elsewhere.

If this policy changes

Before any version of Chapbook starts handling information differently, this page gets a new revision date and an account of what is different, and Apple's privacy label for Chapbook on the App Store is brought into line.
